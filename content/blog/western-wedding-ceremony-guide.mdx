      **First Look**

      The first look is a private moment where the couple sees each other before the ceremony, when they are all dressed, ready, and fully present for the day ahead.

      This is a relatively modern take, especially compared to the traditional Western belief that the couple shouldn’t see each other until they walk down the aisle.

      Today, many couples choose a first look not just for photos, but for what it offers emotionally. It creates space to pause, settle the nerves, and share a quiet moment together before everything begins.

      Some brides also choose to do a first look with their father or bridal party to add another layer of meaning to the day.

      First Touch

      For couples who want to keep the aisle moment as their very first time seeing each other, the first touch is a thoughtful alternative.

      Instead of seeing one another, they connect in a more subtle way like standing back-to-back, separated by a door, or around a corner.

      It’s a moment to exchange a few words, hold hands, or simply be present together without breaking tradition. A small gesture, but often one that feels grounding and intimate.

      **First Dance**

      The first dance is one of the most anticipated moments during a Western wedding reception. It marks the couple’s first shared experience as newlyweds - simple, symbolic, and often emotional.

      Some couples take dance lessons ahead of time to feel more confident, while others keep it completely natural and unchoreographed.

      Whether it happens right after the couple’s entrance, after dinner, or following the cake cutting, the first dance often sets the tone for the rest of the evening. And the song choice? Usually something deeply personal as a reflection of their story together.

      Wedding Toasts

      Wedding toasts are heartfelt (and sometimes hilarious) speeches given to celebrate the couple.

      They often include personal stories, shared memories, and well wishes, offering guests a glimpse into the couple’s journey through the eyes of the people closest to them.

      In traditional Western weddings, the order typically goes: father of the bride, groom, bride, maid of honor, and with the best man closing the speeches.

      Beyond the words themselves, there’s also a symbolic element: the clinking of glasses, believed to ward off bad luck and protect the couple as they begin their new chapter.

      **Cake Cutting**

      The cake cutting is one of the first things the couple does together as husband and wife. This is a small act that symbolizes partnership and shared life ahead.

      After cutting the cake, they’ll usually feed each other a bite and raise a glass of champagne.

      One long-standing Western tradition is saving the top tier of the wedding cake. It’s often kept for a future milestone like the couple’s first anniversary or their child’s christening.

      Another variation you might see is the groom’s cake. Unlike the main wedding cake, this one reflects the groom’s personality that is often playful, personal, and inspired by his hobbies or interests.
