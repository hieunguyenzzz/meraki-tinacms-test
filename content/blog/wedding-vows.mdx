      ***1. Where it all began***

      What was it about your partner that made you fall in love? Maybe it was something big. Or maybe it was something small like the way they laugh, the way they listen, the way they make ordinary days feel lighter. Go back to that moment when you knew. And start from there.

      ***2. How love has changed you***

      Love doesn’t just bring two people together, it shapes them. Think about who you were before, and who you’ve become since. What has this relationship taught you? How has it softened you, challenged you, or helped you grow?

      This is often the part that makes people feel most connected to your story because it’s real.

      3***. What you promise***

      Vows are, at their heart, promises. Not grand, perfect promises but honest ones. Maybe it’s about being there on difficult days. Maybe it’s about building a home that feels safe. Maybe it’s simply about choosing each other, again and again. The simpler it is, the more it stays.

      ***4. The life you imagine together***

      There’s something beautiful about speaking your future out loud. The trips you’ve talked about. The small routines you look forward to. Even the silly plans that only the two of you understand. These details make your vows feel alive, not just meaningful, but yours.

      ***5. Standing together, no matter what***

      Every journey has its challenges. And somewhere in your vows, there’s always a quiet reassurance: that no matter what comes, you won’t have to face it alone. Sometimes, the simplest words carry the most weight.

      **Start earlier than you think**

      Give yourself space, ideally a few weeks before the wedding.

      Not to finish it all at once, but to come back to it. To rewrite. To refine. To let the words settle into something that feels right.

      **Talk to your partner**

      You don’t need to share what you’ve written.

      But it helps to align on tone whether your vows will be lighthearted, emotional, poetic, or simple.

      This avoids that moment where one person writes a novel… and the other writes three lines.

      **Revisit your memories**

      Sometimes, the best way to find words is to go back.

      Look at old photos. Read old messages. Think about the days that mattered.

      You’ll be surprised how naturally the words start to come when you remember how it all felt.

      **Ask yourself the right questions**

      If you feel stuck, try starting here:

      Why did I choose this person?

      When did I know I loved them?

      What have we been through together?

      What has this relationship taught me?

      What do I never want to lose?

      You don’t need to answer everything. Just follow what resonates.

      **Let yourself be inspired**

      A line from a movie. A sentence from a book. Something a friend once said.

      Inspiration is everywhere. What matters is how you make it your own.

      **And when nothing comes…**

      Pause. Don’t force it.

      Sometimes the best thing you can do is step away, rest, and return when your mind is quieter. The right words rarely come when you rush them.
